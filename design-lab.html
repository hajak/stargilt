AETHERFORGE — DESIGN LAB

round 10 · three board layouts × three card layouts · pick one of each

Board A — The Side Bazaar

the market moves to a left rail; the table triples its breathing room

WHY IT WORKS

The table becomes the stage. Kingdom piles live in a compact 2-column rail — always visible, one glance to scan prices. Relics and the Anvil anchor the rail's foot, so acquiring/forging is one region of the screen.

TRADE-OFFS

Tiles carry less info — name, cost, stock; the inspector does the reading (already the buy flow). The hand loses ~300px of width at the fan's widest.

Board B — The Counter

the whole market on one slim 96px strip; the table gains 120px

WHY IT WORKS

Everything stays where you learned it — just compressed. Shop chips show art-thumb, name, cost, stock; kingdom → divider → relics → Anvil, one row, never wraps. Click = the inspector you already buy through.

TRADE-OFFS

No card faces in the shop at rest — you commit to the inspector as the only reading surface. Least dramatic change of the three.

Board C — The Forge Hall

the shop becomes a phase: a grand panel over the table, summoned by the ⚒ button

WHY IT WORKS

Both spaces get everything. At rest the board is pure play — hand, meter, table. A pulsing BAZAAR ⚒1 button (it glows while your ⚒ is unspent) slides the full-size shop over the table: big cards, rack, Anvil, all luxurious. This is Balatro's rhythm: play, then shop, then tally.

TRADE-OFFS

The shop isn't ambient — you can't window-shop while planning a play; one extra click per browse. Boons/evolution reveals need to open the hall themselves.

Cards 1 — Badge Overlay

full-bleed art; the payload floats dead-center ON the graphic

WHY IT WORKS

The card IS the glance. Art fills the frame; big glassy tokens sit mid-card exactly where the eye lands; the ⚡ synergy rides the art's bottom edge. Nothing is hidden, hover adds nothing but zoom. Maximum dopamine, minimum chrome.

TRADE-OFFS

Tokens cover the art's center — sigils read around them. Flavor & typeline retreat entirely to the inspector.

Cards 2 — Split Face

art keeps the top half; a permanent large-type panel owns the bottom

WHY IT WORKS

Never hidden, never overlapping. Art and info each get clean territory: type divider, big pays row, synergy always readable. The most "board game card" of the three — dense, honest, calm.

TRADE-OFFS

Art shrinks to ~half and the card reads as two blocks rather than one object. Least graphic drama.

Cards 3 — Iconic Frame

art as background; a fixed grammar of positions — same slot always means the same thing

WHY IT WORKS

MTG-style anatomy. Cost lives top-right, the big ★ bottom-left, the pays-band crosses the card's waist as a translucent strip, ⚡ directly beneath. After three cards your eye stops searching forever — every card is the same map.

TRADE-OFFS

The waist-band slices the art; very long synergy lines need tight copy. The big ★ gem re-introduces a corner anchor (which we removed in round 7 — here it earns its place as the grammar's fixed point).
